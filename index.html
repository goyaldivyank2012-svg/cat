01 — Why a cat?

More than just a wish.

This isn't a sudden birthday wish or something I've decided on recently. My love for cats has been a part of me for over seven years.

Over time, I've come to understand that having a cat isn't just about cute photographs and cuddles. It's about welcoming a living being into our home, respecting its personality and being there for its needs.

I would love the opportunity to build that kind of bond with a little companion, while making sure it has a safe, comfortable and loving home.

02 — Understanding the responsibility

Love comes with responsibility.

I understand that a cat needs much more than affection. Its health, safety, hygiene and happiness all depend on consistent care.

🥣

Food & fresh water

Daily meals appropriate for its age, along with clean drinking water throughout the day.

🧹

Litter & hygiene

Regular litter maintenance, a clean environment and proper hygiene.

🧶

Play & companionship

Daily interaction, toys, stimulation and time together without forcing affection.

🪮

Grooming & care

Brushing, nail care when needed and attention to coat, ears and general condition.

🩺

Veterinary healthcare

Vaccinations, routine examinations, parasite prevention and professional medical advice.

🏥

Emergency expenses

Being financially prepared for unexpected illness, accidents and urgent treatment.

A commitment measured in years.

Cats can live for 15–20 years or longer. That means caring for a kitten through school, changing routines, holidays and different stages of life. I understand this is not a temporary commitment.

03 — Understanding your perspective

Your Concerns, My Solutions.

I know that bringing a kitten home isn't a small decision. There are practical challenges, and I don't expect you to ignore them. Instead, I'd like to show you how we could work through them together.

CONCERN 01

"Nobody is home when we go out. Who will look after it?"

I understand this is probably one of the biggest challenges. A kitten needs more supervision and companionship than an adult cat, and I wouldn't want it spending long periods without proper care.

Here's what I propose:

  • Before school: I can help with feeding, fresh water, litter cleaning and some playtime before leaving.
  • During school hours: We can arrange a trusted family member or responsible pet sitter to check on the kitten and provide appropriate care.
  • After school: I will make time for play, interaction, feeding according to schedule and general care.
  • During holidays: We can arrange a reliable caregiver in advance rather than leaving the kitten unattended.
  • Unexpected situations: We can identify a backup caregiver before bringing the kitten home.
My promise: We won't bring a kitten home until we have a realistic supervision arrangement that everyone in the family agrees with. If nobody can provide the necessary care, we should wait.
CONCERN 02

"You'll be excited initially, but who will do all the work later?"

I understand why you might worry about this. Getting excited about a kitten is easy. Maintaining a daily routine, even when I'm busy or tired, is the actual responsibility.

I want to make this a proper commitment:

  • Every morning: Check water, prepare meals and maintain the litter area.
  • Every afternoon: Spend dedicated time playing and interacting with the kitten.
  • Every evening: Check food, water, litter and the kitten's general wellbeing.
  • Every week: Help with grooming, cleaning bowls and checking supplies.
  • Every month: Review supplies and discuss health and care requirements with you.

I also propose starting these responsibilities before we even get a kitten.

My idea: Give me a 30-day responsibility challenge. I'll maintain a daily checklist and demonstrate consistency with my existing responsibilities. You can judge my actions rather than just my promises.
CONCERN 03

"A cat is expensive. What about all the expenses?"

I know a cat isn't a one-time purchase. There are recurring expenses and unexpected medical costs, and I understand these need to be considered before making a decision.

We can plan the expenses in advance:

  • Food: Good-quality food suitable for the kitten's age and nutritional needs.
  • Litter: Regular litter purchases and hygiene supplies.
  • Healthcare: Vaccinations, deworming, preventive treatments and routine veterinary examinations.
  • Initial setup: Litter box, carrier, scratching post, toys, bowls and bedding.
  • Emergency fund: Money reserved for unexpected illness, accidents or treatment.
My proposal: Before adopting, we can research actual local prices, prepare a monthly budget and establish an emergency veterinary fund. I don't expect you to take on an expense without understanding what it involves.
CONCERN 04

"What happens when we travel or go on holiday?"

Family trips shouldn't become a problem for the kitten, and the kitten shouldn't become a reason we cannot make family plans.

Here's how we could prepare:

  • Arrange a trusted pet sitter or experienced caregiver.
  • Prepare written feeding and care instructions.
  • Provide the caregiver with veterinary contact details.
  • Make sure someone can respond to emergencies.
  • Discuss suitable boarding options with our vet if necessary.
We will arrange care before travelling. An automatic feeder or water fountain can help with routine needs, but cannot replace a person checking on the kitten.
CONCERN 05

"What about our furniture, cleanliness and the house?"

I understand that a kitten can scratch furniture, shed fur and occasionally make a mess. Keeping the home comfortable for everyone matters.

Some practical preparations:

  • Provide scratching posts and appropriate toys.
  • Keep the litter box in a suitable, accessible location.
  • Brush the kitten according to its coat requirements.
  • Clean accidents properly and maintain litter hygiene.
  • Use safe spaces and gradually introduce the kitten to the home.
  • Keep dangerous plants, chemicals, wires and small objects out of reach.
We can kitten-proof the house before it arrives. I will also help maintain the cleanliness of the areas the kitten uses.
CONCERN 06

"What if you lose interest or your routine changes?"

This is something I have thought about seriously. A cat could live for 15–20 years or even longer. School, exams, college and other parts of life will change during that time.

My understanding:

  • Care cannot stop when school becomes busy.
  • We need a family backup plan for exam periods.
  • Moving or travelling must include the cat's needs.
  • Medical care cannot be postponed because it is inconvenient.
  • The responsibility continues after the initial excitement fades.
I understand that I am 13 and cannot independently manage every aspect of pet ownership. This would be a family commitment, with me taking an active and consistent role in everyday care.

I'm not asking you to take a leap of faith.

I'm asking for an opportunity to demonstrate responsibility, prepare a proper care plan and discuss whether we can make this work together. We don't have to rush into anything.

05 — Choosing the right kitten

The right kitten, not just any kitten.

Finding a kitten should be a careful decision. Its health and welfare will always matter more than its appearance or a particular date.

✓

Veterinary examination

A qualified veterinarian should examine the kitten before adoption or purchase.

✓

Health and vaccination records

Check medical history, vaccination records, deworming and any previous treatment.

✓

Appropriate age

A kitten should not be separated from its mother too early. Around 12–14 weeks is a sensible target where possible, with individual veterinary guidance.

✓

Responsible background

Ask about the parents, health history, socialisation and conditions in which the kitten was raised.

✓

No impulse purchases

We should avoid questionable sellers, unhealthy breeding practices and rushed decisions.

I may especially love Ragdolls, but I would be open to the kitten that is genuinely right for our family. A healthy, well-socialised companion matters most.
